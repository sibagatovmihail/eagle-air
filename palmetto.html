HVAC service in Palmetto, Florida

Palmetto sits on the north bank of the Manatee River in Manatee County, directly across from Bradenton, and EagleAir serves it as part of its northern service area. Much of Palmetto's housing stock predates the region's newer subdivisions, which means a lot of systems here are working against ductwork and insulation designed to a much older standard.

In practice that shows up as a system that runs constantly without ever satisfying the thermostat. Before recommending a larger unit, EagleAir checks what the existing one is fighting: duct leakage into the attic, a return that is too small for the blower, and a coil that has not been cleaned in years all produce the same symptom as an undersized system, and all three are cheaper to fix than a replacement.

Questions

Palmetto HVAC questions, answered

Answers for Palmetto homeowners in Manatee County — from an HVAC contractor that works this area.

Is my older Palmetto home's ductwork worth replacing?

It depends on what a duct-leakage test shows. A typical duct system loses roughly 20 to 30 percent of the air it moves, and in a Florida attic that lost air is replaced by hot, humid attic air pulled in through return leaks. Sealing existing ducts is often enough. Full replacement makes sense when the runs are undersized, crushed, or have deteriorated to the point that sealing will not hold.

How often should an air conditioner be serviced in Florida?

Twice a year. Florida systems run close to year-round, so a Sarasota AC accumulates far more runtime hours than the same unit in a northern state where cooling season lasts three or four months. A spring visit before peak load and a second visit in autumn catch the failures that actually strand people: a clogged condensate drain, a weak start capacitor, low refrigerant, and a coil packed with dust. EagleAir maintenance visits cover coil cleaning, drain-line flushing, refrigerant pressure check, electrical and capacitor inspection, and a thermostat calibration.

Why is my air conditioner leaking water inside the house?

A clogged condensate drain line is the cause in the large majority of Florida cases. Your air handler pulls moisture out of humid indoor air and drains it away; in Florida's humidity, algae and biological slime grow inside that line and block it, so water backs up into the drain pan and overflows. A secondary cause is a frozen evaporator coil that melts all at once. Shut the system off at the thermostat to stop more water, and call for service. Left alone, an overflowing pan damages drywall, ceilings and the air handler itself.

How long does an air conditioner last in Florida?

Ten to fifteen years, which is shorter than the fifteen to twenty years often quoted nationally. The reason is runtime: a Sarasota system may run most of the year, so it reaches the same wear as a northern unit in far fewer calendar years. On the barrier islands — Longboat Key, Siesta Key — salt air corrodes outdoor condenser coils and can cut that further. Systems near the coast benefit from more frequent coil rinsing and, at replacement time, from coastal-rated or coated coils.

Should I repair my AC or replace it?

The practical rule of thumb: if the system is under ten years old and the repair costs less than a third of a new system, repair it. If it is over twelve years old, uses discontinued R-22 refrigerant, or the compressor has failed, replacement is usually the better value. A failed compressor on an older unit is the clearest replace signal, because the part cost approaches a new system's price. EagleAir quotes both options with flat-rate pricing before any work begins, so the choice is yours and the numbers are on the table.

A no-cool call in August in Florida is a health issue, not an inconvenience, particularly for older residents and households with infants.
