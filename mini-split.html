Mini-Split Systems Installation

Ductless mini-split systems deliver targeted cooling and heating to individual rooms without the cost and disruption of running new ductwork. They're an ideal solution for home additions, converted garages, sunrooms, and older homes that were built without central air. Our installations include a full load assessment to match the right unit capacity to your space, professional mounting with sealed refrigerant lines, and a post-install performance check to confirm everything runs as expected.

How it works

What a mini-split installation involves and how to tell it fits your space

A ductless installation is designed room by room. EagleAir load-calculates the specific space rather than the whole house, because a head that is too large for one room short-cycles and leaves it cool and damp in exactly the way an oversized central system does. Placement follows the calculation: the indoor head goes high on a wall with a clear throw across the room, the outdoor unit sits on a pad or a wall bracket with service clearance around it, and a single sleeved core drill through the exterior wall carries the refrigerant line set, the condensate drain and the power cable.

The part of the job that decides how long the system lasts is invisible once the covers are on. Flare connections are cut, deburred and torqued to the manufacturer's specification, brazed joints are made under a dry nitrogen purge so no oxide scale forms inside the tubing, the circuit is pressure-tested with nitrogen, and it is then evacuated below 500 microns and confirmed on a micron gauge before the charge is released and adjusted for the actual line-set length. Condensate has to reach a proper termination, by gravity where the fall allows and by condensate pump where it does not. A single-zone installation is usually finished in a day.

A mini-split is the right answer when the space is one the central system was never sized to serve: a converted garage, a room addition, an enclosed lanai, a workshop, a home office that runs hot through the afternoon, or an older Florida house that never had ductwork at all. It also earns its place wherever one room should simply be held at a different temperature from the rest. It is the wrong answer when the whole house is struggling, because that is a load, charge or duct problem, and hanging a head in one room treats a symptom. Questions

Mini-Split Systems questions, answered

Common mini-split systems questions from Sarasota, Manatee and Charlotte county customers.

When does a ductless mini-split make more sense than central AC?

Mini-splits are the better answer for spaces the central system was never sized to reach: a converted garage, a room addition, an enclosed lanai, a home office, a workshop, or an older Florida house that never had ductwork. They are also useful where one room runs consistently hot — a west-facing room taking afternoon sun — because they cool that zone without over-cooling the rest of the house.

Are mini-splits more efficient than central air conditioning?

Generally yes, for two reasons. There are no ducts, so the 20 to 30 percent of conditioned air that a typical duct system leaks into an attic is not lost at all. And mini-splits use inverter-driven compressors that modulate output continuously instead of cycling on and off at full power, which both saves energy and removes more humidity — a real advantage in Florida.

How many indoor units can one outdoor unit run?

A multi-zone mini-split outdoor unit typically supports two to five indoor heads, each with its own thermostat and remote. Zoning this way means unused rooms are not being cooled. The right configuration depends on a load calculation per room, not on room count alone — EagleAir sizes the outdoor unit to the combined load and confirms it before installation.

Do mini-splits heat as well as cool in a Florida winter?

Yes. A mini-split is a heat pump, and it heats by running its refrigeration cycle in reverse. Inverter-driven mini-splits produce useful heat well below the temperatures a Southwest Florida winter reaches, and because a heat pump moves heat rather than generating it, it delivers several units of heat for each unit of electricity where an electric resistance strip delivers one. For a garage conversion, an enclosed lanai or a room addition, that makes a single mini-split head a complete year-round solution rather than a cooling-only appliance.

How much maintenance does a mini-split need?

Rinse the washable filters in each indoor head monthly, and have the head's blower wheel, coil and drain pan professionally cleaned once a year. A mini-split has no ducts and no large central filter, so dust and Florida humidity collect on the indoor coil and blower wheel instead; a head that develops a musty smell or starts dripping is telling you that service is due. The outdoor unit needs two feet of clearance and a periodic coil rinse, more often on properties near the Gulf where salt film accelerates corrosion. Holding indoor relative humidity in the 45 to 55 percent range does most of the work of keeping mould out of the head.
