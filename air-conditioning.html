How it works

What an AC repair visit involves, and when you need one

An EagleAir air conditioning repair starts with measurement, not parts. The technician records the temperature split between the return air and the supply air, reads suction and liquid pressures against the design charge for that model, measures the run capacitor against its rated microfarads, then checks the contactor, the blower amp draw, the filter, and the condensate drain and its float switch. That order matters, because the same symptom, a house that will not get cold, comes from a dozen different faults and several of the most common ones are inexpensive parts. You are given a flat-rate price for the repair before any work begins.

Expect a residential visit to take one to three hours, and expect the technician to need access to both the indoor air handler, usually in a closet or garage, and the outdoor condenser. Power is isolated at the outdoor disconnect before anything is opened: a run capacitor stores a dangerous charge even after the breaker is off, which is the main reason AC diagnosis is not a homeowner job. If the diagnosis points toward replacement rather than repair, that quote is free and carries no obligation.

Three signs say a repair call is due rather than optional. First, air from the vents that is not clearly colder than the room; a healthy system typically drops the air 16 to 22 degrees Fahrenheit across the evaporator coil, and a smaller split points to low charge or a fouled coil. Second, a house that reaches temperature but still feels clammy: indoor relative humidity should sit between 45 and 55 percent, and a system that cannot hold that is short-cycling, low on refrigerant or oversized for the load. Third, anything involving water or ice, such as a wet air handler closet or frost on the copper suction line, means the system should be switched off now, because running it in that state is how compressors die.

Air Conditioning questions, answered

Common air conditioning questions from Sarasota, Manatee and Charlotte county customers.

What are the most common AC repairs in Sarasota?

Capacitor failure, clogged condensate drain lines, refrigerant leaks and dirty coils make up the bulk of Sarasota AC repair calls. Capacitors fail from heat and age and are among the least expensive parts to replace. Drain lines clog because Florida humidity feeds algae growth inside the line. Refrigerant is never consumed, so a low charge always means a leak that must be found rather than simply topped up. EagleAir works on all major brands including Carrier, Trane, Lennox and Goodman.

How long does an AC repair take?

Most residential AC repairs are completed in a single visit of one to three hours. Capacitor and contactor replacement, drain-line clearing and thermostat faults are typically same-visit fixes. Repairs that need an ordered part — a specific compressor, a coil, or a control board for an older model — take longer, and EagleAir quotes the flat rate and the timeline before the work starts.

Do you repair commercial air conditioning?

Yes. EagleAir services both residential and commercial systems across Sarasota, Manatee and Charlotte counties, including rooftop package units and split systems for shops, offices, restaurants and rental properties. Commercial calls are scheduled around business hours where possible, and 24/7 emergency coverage applies to commercial customers as well.

Why is my AC leaking water?

A clogged condensate drain line is the most common cause of water around a Florida air handler. An air conditioner pulls moisture out of the air continuously in this climate, and that water leaves through a drain line where warmth, darkness and humidity let algae grow until the line blocks; the pan then overflows, or the float switch shuts the system down to stop it. The other frequent cause is a frozen evaporator coil melting, which points back to a dirty filter, a blocked return or a refrigerant leak. Turn the system off at the thermostat when you find water, check the filter, and call before the ceiling or the air handler cabinet takes damage.

Should I repair or replace my air conditioner?

Repair usually wins under about ten years of age, and replacement usually wins past that point or once the compressor has failed. Air conditioners in Florida typically last 10 to 15 years because they run for most of the calendar year rather than a single season. Two other things push toward replacement: a system losing refrigerant repeatedly, since refrigerant is never consumed and a low charge always means a leak in a sealed circuit, and equipment old enough that its efficiency sits far below the 14.3 SEER2 minimum that has applied to new split-system central air conditioners in the Southeast region since 1 January 2023. EagleAir quotes replacement free and without obligation, so the two numbers can be compared side by side.
